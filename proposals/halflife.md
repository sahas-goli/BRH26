# Halflife - Every answer has an age

**Recommendation: build an iMessage venue guide that answers only from unexpired evidence. When its evidence has expired it says "I don't know", sends a nearby opted-in person on a 20-second check, and texts the asker the answer when it arrives.**

Researched 2026-10-02 for a team of three software engineers and one hardware person. Priorities below reflect sponsor fit, build cost, and how the demonstration plays; they are not predicted wins.

## The idea

**Who:** attendees at venues where conditions change by the minute: hackathons, conferences, campus libraries, dining halls. The first users are BigRed//Hacks attendees, live this weekend.

**Problem:** venue facts go stale in minutes. Is the CAD workshop still in Gates 114? Is there still food? Maps do not know, announcements lag, and an LLM guide repeats old facts confidently. This is the freshness half of Jon Kleinberg's open problem in the [Navigation theme notes](../resources/02-theme-navigation.md): the LLM that named places closed since 2018. Correctness is the other half.

**How it works:**

1. **Evidence ledger.** Sensor posts (tray fill level, activity) and human reports land in one Tiger Data hypertable: time, zone, claim, value, source, reporter. Gemini turns a free-text reply or photo into a typed claim.
2. **Expiry per claim.** Each claim type has an expiry window (food 10 min, queue 5 min, room schedule 60 min, layout never). Once enough history exists, each zone and claim pair also shows its *observed* change interval from the venue's own data, with the sample count. This informs the operator; defaults stay in force for the gate.
3. **Hard gate.** The agent asserts only evidence younger than its expiry, and every fact carries its source and age. Expired or conflicting evidence produces a refusal.
4. **Refusal triggers a refresh.** A sensor covering the claim is read first. Otherwise a 20-second sidequest goes to the opted-in attendee with the freshest check-in in that zone. Each person gets at most one open sidequest and one per 10 minutes. After a 90-second timeout it tries the next person, then tells the asker "no one available" along with the last known value, explicitly marked expired.
5. **Deferred delivery.** The refreshed answer arrives unprompted in the original asker's thread.
6. **Audit.** Every sent answer is logged with the evidence it cited. An as-of query recomputes whether each citation was unexpired at send time, so the stat on screen is *stale assertions: 0* over real traffic.
7. **The model never writes a fact.** Gemini only interprets messages and photos. Numbers, ages, and directions come from templates filled from the database. Each check-in post encodes its position and the facing of its wall, so "the door on your left" is computed from venue geometry, never generated by a model. This avoids Kleinberg's left/right error by construction instead of correcting it afterwards.
8. **Physical freshness.** A 3D-printed venue map lights each zone while its knowledge is fresh and fades it as the knowledge ages.

Halflife is about information freshness only: nothing is physically repaired. That separates it from the team's [RouteRepair](route-repair.md) proposal. Pitch one or the other, never both.

## The two-minute demonstration

Table: a snack tray on a sensor post, a QR check-in post, the LED relief map, a laptop showing the live map and audit panel. While the judge approaches, a teammate adds their number in the Photon dashboard (about 15 seconds). If that fails, the judge uses a team phone.

1. **0:00 Hook.** "Every answer here has an age. When the age runs out, Halflife stops answering and goes to find out."
2. **0:10 Refusal.** The judge scans the QR post and texts "is the CAD workshop still on?" Halflife replies with the route ("Gates 114: exit through the door on your left, 40 m"), then: "My last report is 74 min old, past the 60-minute limit. I don't know. Asking someone there now." Gates is dark on the relief map.
3. **0:30 Sensor beat.** While waiting, the judge asks "is there still food here?" and gets "Yes, about 70% full (sensor, 4 s ago)." The judge takes half the snacks, asks again, and gets "About 30% (sensor, 2 s ago)." The table zone stays fully lit because the sensor keeps its knowledge fresh.
4. **0:50 Payoff.** A teammate standing in Gates receives the sidequest and replies with a photo. Gemini extracts the claim, and **the judge's phone buzzes unprompted**: "Update: the CAD workshop is still running in Gates 114, about 15 people (attendee photo, 8 s ago)." Gates lights up and begins to fade.
5. **1:15 Proof.** The replay slider runs over real history since Saturday morning, showing the observed change intervals ("snack table: changes about every 9 min, n=41"). The audit panel reads, for example: real users, answers sent, **stale assertions 0**, refusals, completed refreshes, median evidence age. It also shows Tiger performance numbers.
6. **1:50 Close.** "Maps can't talk, and LLMs can't be trusted with facts. Halflife refuses, asks, and tells you when it knows."

The payoff is an answer that did not exist when the judge asked, delivered later to the judge's own phone, with proof the system never stated an expired fact. The teammate's role is staged, so the audit panel must show real overnight sidequests answered by real attendees.

### Integrations to skip

- **SpaceX:** Cursor plus Grok Voice or Imagine would add a second interface with no job in the loop.
- **ElevenLabs:** a voice adds nothing to a text-thread product, and a second channel splits the demo.
- **Presage:** physiology has no role in evidence freshness.
- **Nessie:** paying responders would add a bounty-economy story and invite gaming.
- **Solana:** the audit already lives in Tiger; a single venue operator needs no shared settlement.
- **Beginner:** only if every teammate is at their first hackathon. **DGX Spark:** only as a local vision fallback if Gemini quota fails.

## Originality analysis

**Qualified pass on the subfeature test.** Every component already exists, but no reviewed product runs the whole loop: refuse when stale, dispatch, deliver later, then prove nothing stale was said. Adding that loop to any of these products would make it a different product, so the loop must stay the visible core.

| Existing product or research | Existing capability | Required differentiation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Microsoft patent US8983976](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8983976) | Crowd content that expires, lifetimes weighted by evidence quality, "still there?" prompts to opted-in nearby users. | Expiry gates what an agent may tell a specific asker; refusal triggers a dispatch whose answer returns to that asker's thread; an audit proves the gate held. |
| [Waze / Google "still there?" reports](https://support.google.com/androidauto/answer/6348166?hl=en) | Report lifetimes adjusted by votes; passers-by confirm. | Question-driven, indoors at one venue, with per-claim expiry, sensor evidence, and a deferred answer. |
| [Google Ask Maps](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-immersive-navigation/) | Conversational "right now" place answers; Gemini parses photo contributions. | Show evidence age, refuse when stale, and route the question to a person who is there. |
| [HumanPing](https://humanping.io/) | An agent pays a nearby worker to verify a place with a timestamped photo. | Dispatch fires automatically from the expiry gate, goes to opted-in peers ranked by check-in freshness, and is delivered to the original asker. |
| [Mappedin + Satisfi venue assistant](https://www.mappedin.com/resources/news/miami-international-airport-launches-ai-conversational-agents/) | Conversational indoor venue assistant with operational data and map directions. | Age on every answer, refusal when stale, and a sensor-or-human refresh loop. |
| [Occuspace Octi](https://www.occuspace.com/octi) | Conversational, sourced answers over occupancy sensors, for facilities staff. | Attendee-facing, fuses human evidence, and gates on expiry. |
| [Frood](https://www.frood.app/) | Expiring "is there still food?" posts updated by people. | Food is only the hardware beat; never pitch it as the idea. |
| [Mahmud et al., selecting strangers to answer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.2013) | Routing location questions to strangers at a place, with per-person ask limits. | Do not claim the dispatcher as novel; claim that it is triggered by the expiry gate. |
| [FreshCache](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04281) | Freshness-gated answer reuse for RAG caches, with refresh on expiry. | Apply the gate to physical venue state, refreshed by sensors and people rather than by re-retrieval. |
| [Floorplan2Guide](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12177v1) | Scanned markers plus LLM first-person indoor directions. | Not claimed. Halflife's directions are templated from geometry and facing. |
| [Terp4you iMessage campus agent](https://anthropic-x-maryland-hackathon.devpost.com/project-gallery) | iMessage campus Q&A agents are a recurring hackathon genre. | Defined by what it refuses to say and how it repairs that. |
| [StudyScape](https://scholarcommons.scu.edu/idp_senior/115/) | Sensor nodes feeding a live campus zone map. | The map shows how fresh the knowledge is, not occupancy. |
| [Kleinberg's BRH26 open problem](../resources/02-theme-navigation.md) | Several teams will demonstrate left/right fixes and grounded LLM directions. | Lead with refusal, dispatch, and the unprompted answer, which other teams are unlikely to build. |

Do not claim novelty for food or activity sensing, the facing-encoded posts (orientation-code prior art was not checked), or the dispatcher on its own. This is a bounded search of the sources above, not proof of novelty or a patent opinion.

## Success criteria and risks

- Stale assertions stay at 0 across every real answer, by the audit query rather than by assertion.
- Refusal, dispatch, and unprompted delivery to the judge's phone complete in under a minute, every rehearsal.
- A judge's physical action changes the answer within seconds.
- Real attendees asked and answered questions overnight; counts come from the database, not estimates.
- Tiger performance numbers are measured and shown, with synthetic data labeled as such.
- With nobody available, the asker hears "no one available" and the last known value marked expired.
- The demonstration fits in two minutes.

**Risks.** Photon shared-line limits cap traction; Telegram is the overflow and the judge's number is registered at the table. Adoption needs organizer support; without it, traction is the team plus friends, and the pitch says so. A knocked-over post goes stale and the agent refuses, which is the intended behavior. Gaming is limited by rate limits, reporter identity on every row, and the rule that conflicting evidence causes a refusal instead of picking a winner. Privacy: zone-level self check-in only, check-ins expire after 20 minutes, "stop" deletes the person's data, and nobody is tracked. One night of history is thin, so observed intervals always show their sample count. Small stakes ("is there still pizza?") can undersell the idea, so pitch the loop, generalized to libraries, dining halls, and conference venues.

Protect the core demonstration: the system refuses because its evidence expired, finds out from a person, and delivers the answer to the judge's phone without being asked again.
